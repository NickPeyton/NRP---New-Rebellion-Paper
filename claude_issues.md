## Table 1 — Research Weaknesses, Data Gaps, and Required Analyses

| # | Weakness | Data Required | Analysis Required |
|---|----------|---------------|-------------------|
| 1 | **Entry fines mechanism untestable.** Central causal chain is Dissolution → fear of entry fine hikes → rebellion, but no entry fine data exists. Monastic land is used as proxy with no validation. | Entry fine records (manorial rolls, estate surveys). Some survive in National Archives (SC 2, DL 29 series). Even a sparse cross-section would help. | Regression of entry fines on monastic land to validate proxy; ideally an IV or mediation model if data permits. |
| 2 | **Large-house vs. small-house effect is a key finding but is not written up or formally tested.** Abstract and conclusion claim large-house land predicts rebellion more strongly, but there is no dedicated results section, no interaction test, and no historical explanation of the mechanism in the text. | None required — data already exists (`lsm_arak`, `lbg_arak`, `smHouse`, `bigHouse`). | Formal interaction test (large × dissolution threat) or separate stratum regressions; dedicated results subsection. |
| 3 | **On-site vs. off-site land split not written up.** A results table (`primary_split.tex`) apparently exists but no text explains it. This distinction is critical: off-site land means tenants, which is the economic mechanism. | None required. | Results subsection explaining the on-site/off-site split coefficients and why they differ. |
| 4 | **Monastic order not exploited.** Cistercians, Carthusians, and Benedictines are discussed in the historiography as having different relationships with local communities, but no regression distinguishes them. | Order classification for each house — partially in existing `NationalArchivesData.csv`; may need completion. | Regression with order fixed effects or separate dummies; F-test for joint significance. |
| 5 | **Friary analysis incomplete.** `friary` dummy appears in regression tables but is not discussed. Mendicant orders had a distinct social role (urban preaching, alms distribution) not captured by the Valor land variables. | None required beyond existing `friarPoints.shp`. | Separate coefficient discussion; potentially a sub-analysis for parishes with friary presence only. |
| 6 | **Elite vs. commons comparison exists in code but never written up.** `elite_vs_commons.R` runs but outputs to console only; McFadden R² comparison is never discussed in the paper. | None required. | Add stargazer table output; write up a comparison subsection. |
| 7 | **Crown pressure on abbots unmeasured.** Paper argues large abbeys were under active Royal pressure in 1535–36 but cannot quantify this. It is the proposed explanation for why large-house land predicts rebellion despite large houses not yet being dissolved. | Abbatial election interference records (*Letters and Papers*, Henry VIII, vols. VIII–XI). A binary "Crown-pressured abbot" variable would suffice. | Include as a regressor or instrument; test whether its exclusion inflates the large-house land coefficient. |
| 8 | **Individual-level rebel data unused.** An 89-name Lincolnshire Rebels list exists but does not appear in any analysis. It could validate whether rebels are monastic tenants, taxpayers, or have family ties to monastic houses. | Ideally a match to Valor counterparty names or lay subsidy records. Even matching surnames to monastic tenant rolls would be informative. | Cross-tabulation; logit on individual rebellion given monastic tenant status. Even descriptive statistics would strengthen the argument. |
| 9 | **"Old vs. new grievances" analysis listed in TODO but never done.** This would directly address whether pre-existing tensions vs. Dissolution-specific threat better explains rebellion. | No new data required — existing variables can be re-categorised as pre-1536 structural (old) vs. Dissolution-specific (new). | Nested model comparison with McFadden R²; LR test on variable blocks. |
| 10 | **News travel speed assumptions unvalidated.** Survival analysis uses 20/50/100 km/day speeds with no sensitivity analysis or check against dated muster records. | Dated contemporary accounts of news spread (*Letters and Papers* contains many dated dispatches). | Sensitivity analysis over plausible speed ranges; ideally a validation regression of predicted exposure day against observed muster date. |
| 11 | **Gentleman exogeneity assumed, not tested.** Percy proximity is treated as exogenous because of the inheritance dispute, but this is asserted rather than demonstrated. If Percy territory overlapped systematically with monastic land, the Percy coefficient absorbs monastic effects. | Pre-rebellion documentary evidence of Percy grievance being independent of monastic concerns (*Letters and Papers*). | Partial correlation between Percy proximity and monastic land variables; sensitivity analysis dropping Percy entirely; IV if a valid instrument can be found. |
| 12 | **Subgroup / terrain interaction absent.** Upland vs. lowland parishes had very different monastic roles (pastoral vs. arable economies, different alms patterns). No interaction terms exist. | None required. | Interaction terms (monastic vars × upland/lowland); or separate regressions by terrain type. |

## Table 2 — Other Issues

| # | Category | Issue |
|---|----------|-------|
| 1 | **Writing** | Results section is incomplete for ~40% of the analyses that have been run. Tables for grid regressions, Conley SEs, distance-weighted models, and the full-model robustness check all exist but have no corresponding text. A referee would flag this immediately. |
| 2 | **Writing** | Discussion section is approximately 2.5 pages for a complex multi-method paper. Mechanisms (tenure fear, employment, alms, hospitality, religious threat) are mentioned but not argued for or against using the results. |
| 3 | **Writing** | No dedicated Methods section. Regression specifications are introduced inline in Results. Survival model assumptions (proportional hazards, censoring), PSM algorithm choices, and IPW overlap conditions are not described anywhere. |
| 4 | **Writing** | Sample size N is never stated explicitly in the paper. Number of rebellion events (primary musters) is also not stated. These are basic descriptive statistics a referee will request immediately. |
| 5 | **Methods** | Proportional hazards assumption is not tested in `survival_analysis.R`. Schoenfeld residual tests or scaled residual plots should be added before the survival results are reported. |
| 6 | **Methods** | PSM balance diagnostics (`ipw_balance_plot.png`) exist but are not discussed in the paper. Overlap/positivity (common support) is not checked or reported for any propensity score model. |
| 7 | **Methods** | Multiple hypothesis testing: 30+ model specifications tested across three outcomes, multiple land variable forms, four grid sizes, five PSM thresholds, and several distance cutoffs. No p-value correction is applied or even discussed. |
| 8 | **Methods** | The 12.5 km distance-decay threshold for weighted variables and the 20 km gentleman proximity buffer are stated but not justified with sensitivity analyses. A referee is very likely to ask about these. |
| 9 | **Methods** | Spatial autocorrelation: Conley SEs are computed but only at fixed distance cutoffs. Moran's I on regression residuals is not computed. Spatial lag or spatial error models are not tested as alternatives. |
| 10 | **Code / Reproducibility** | Eight R scripts are now obsolete after the recent unification (`parish_logits_interactions.R`, `progressive_regression_plots.R`, `progressive_regression_plots_landOwned.R`, `progressive_regression_plots_ownOther.R`, `regression_plots_landOwned.R`, `survival_plots.R`, `AIPW_plots.R`, `AIPW_plots_ownOther.R`) but remain in `Code/`. They will confuse anyone running the codebase and may get run by mistake. |
| 11 | **Code / Reproducibility** | `northParishFlows.shp` must be regenerated before the updated R scripts will run — `lni_arak`, `lown_arak`, and `loth_arak` do not yet exist in the saved shapefile. There is no README or pipeline script making this run-order dependency explicit. |
| 12 | **Data integrity** | `elite_vs_commons.R` uses `distScot` as a geographic control while some other scripts have used `Y_COORD` at various points. Worth auditing that all scripts use a consistent control specification. |
| 13 | **Argument** | The paper does not engage with the possibility that large-house land predicts rebellion because of *religious* threat (extinction of major monasteries = attack on the faith) rather than *economic* threat (tenant disruption). These two interpretations are observationally equivalent given current data, and the distinction is central to the Hoyle vs. Bush debate. A robustness discussion is needed even if it cannot be resolved empirically. |
| 14 | **Argument** | The presentation claims "first econometric evidence" on Pilgrimage causes. This strong claim needs to be qualified: the analysis is cross-sectional, the mechanism cannot be directly tested, and confounding by unmeasured local politics and family networks is acknowledged but not bounded. A reviewer will push back hard without clear caveats. |
| 15 | **Presentation vs. paper gap** | The presentation is reportedly up to date while the paper is not — meaning claims in slides may not yet be written into the paper, and visualisations may not be referenced in the text. These need to be reconciled before submission. |
